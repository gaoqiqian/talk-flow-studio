# Speech and reference analysis

## Inspect the source

Record duration, orientation, audio presence, portrait position and source frame rate. Listen to the speech, not only the thumbnail. Watch reference action sequences at native pace where feasible; use contact sheets to index the whole reference and closer samples for tricky actions. If only sampled frames were inspected, say so.

Record reference traits as observations: opening rhythm, shot balance, alignment, focal hierarchy, typography, motion verbs, information builds, proof treatment and ending. Separate these observations from the new storyboard. A recording of a video player may include loops or UI; isolate the actual content region.

## Transcription and cuts

Use a timestamped transcript with start, end and text; word timing is preferable for emphasis. Verify important names, numbers and product claims by listening. Do not substitute confidence scores for manual checking.

An edit decision list is an ordered list of retained source windows with sourceStart and sourceEnd. Do not reorder unintentionally. Final time is the sum of preceding retained durations plus the offset in the current window. A word removed by a cut has no final time. A segment straddling a cut must split; the map tool outputs segment intersections, which require caption copy review because whole-sentence text may no longer fit each fragment.

Preserve breathing room around sensitive cuts; check both the visible jump and consonants at the audio seam. Do not add silence trimming by default. Preserve gestures and moments of personality.

## Beat timing

Anchor a headline to the phrase that introduces its idea. Start evidence shortly before or at the associated claim; hold it long enough to read. A practical initial timing budget for Chinese is about 4–6 characters per second, English about 2–3 words per second; then adjust by listening and content complexity. These are editorial heuristics, not hard gates.

Write exact cue times for step reveals and chat turns. Use a short, decisive entrance (often 0.25–0.55 seconds), then let reading and speech carry the shot. Leave time to understand the previous step before revealing a new one. Avoid two simultaneous hero entrances.

## Hook and proof

Use an observable situation, surprising contrast in the user's story, or concrete problem. Scores and achievements act as brief credibility evidence when relevant; avoid turning the opening into a list of awards. Product proof should show the claimed interaction or state. Label screenshots as screenshots and simulated dialogue as an illustration. A generated practice question must not be presented as a verified real question.
